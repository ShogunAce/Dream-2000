# Dream 2000 No's Tracker (name still open)

A hopecore-cyberpunk outreach dashboard that turns brand outreach into a game: every "no" earns XP, every rare "yes" earns a trophy.

## The Unique Kernel
Rejection is rewarded instead of feared. Sending an ask and getting a "no" pays out XP and moves a level bar toward the 2000-no goal. A "yes" is a rare event that unlocks a trophy animation. A normal outreach CRM treats a "no" as failure; this one makes it progress.

## Who It's For
The learner: a tech/hackathon/vibecoding creator with about 1000 followers across six platforms, now growing X and Threads. They believe brands won't work with them because they're too small. Today they track asks in a spreadsheet. They want to build the habit of putting themselves out there and get used to rejection.

## The Core Loop
Open the tracker and see today's outreach missions (brands to contact) plus an inspiring quote. Pick a brand, review the message, copy it, send it elsewhere, and check it off. The brand moves from "uncontacted" to "contacted / awaiting response." When a reply arrives, log it as a no (XP) or a yes (XP and a trophy), and watch the level bar climb. They come back for the daily missions and the progression.

## Inspiration & Identity
"An astronaut floating through a colorful cosmos, but protected." It should feel like having it open while vibecoding at 1am, listening to drum and bass, building the future. Cyberpunk 2077 but hopecore. Quotes from figures like Maya Angelou, Seneca, Langston Hughes, Malcolm Gladwell, David Goggins, Malcolm X. Long-term inspiration: a "Jarvis" command center for all their businesses. The learner has image references to share at `3-prd`.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
"I have a limiting belief that brands won't work with me because I'm too small." The plan is to chase 2000 no's while posting, competing in hackathons, and eventually hosting their own. "You never know what kind of yes's I will get."

## What "Working" Looks Like
A one-minute demo: open the app, see a list of brands to reach out to (loaded from their spreadsheet) and a quote. Review a message, copy it, send it, and check it off. An animation moves the brand from uncontacted to contacted / awaiting response. Simulate a "no" and show XP gain. Simulate a "yes" and show XP gain plus a trophy animation. The level bar shows progress. The "oh, that's cool" beat is the trophy animation after a yes, in the cosmic aesthetic.

## The POC Boundary
- Daily outreach missions populated from the learner's existing spreadsheet (brands, messages, other info)
- Brand view with message, copy-to-clipboard, and a done check-off
- Status flow: uncontacted → contacted / awaiting response → no or yes
- XP for no's, rare trophy for yes, and a level/progress bar toward 2000
- Daily quote
- The cosmic hopecore-cyberpunk look
- A simple landing page that explains the project, with a button into the dashboard (no account or login). The learner found an existing site that fits and will adapt it with their own images and make it meaningfully theirs. It is not a scroll-driven site. Source and license of that site are to be confirmed at `3-prd`. Animation assets may be generated through ElevenLabs and motion design skills
- A dashboard that feels like a personal MySpace (a personalized space, not a generic dashboard, and without any friends concept). The learner can't fully articulate this yet, so `3-prd` explores it with their image references

## Later
- An agent that sends the outreach automatically
- Tracking across all platforms, and progress over time beyond the level bar
- Becoming a broader Jarvis-style command center for all their businesses
- A way for anyone to build and customize their own version

## Explicitly Cut
- Agent-driven sending: out of PoC reach; manual copy-and-send proves the loop
- Multi-user accounts or customizable builds for others: the PoC is for one person
- Controlling other businesses from the dashboard: the tracker is the first room, not the whole Jarvis
- Accounts and login: the landing page button goes straight to the dashboard
- Social or friends features: the MySpace feel is about personal customization only
